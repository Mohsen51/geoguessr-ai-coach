## Identifying Slovenia

- The Slovenian landscape is generally very **hilly and forested**, with occasional agriculture. _For exceptions to this, see the region guessing section._
- A very significant portion of the coverage in Slovenia was taken on small roads deep **inside forest**.
- Slovenian architecture takes much inspiration from alpine architecture, with light coloured walls, **dark wooden details** and semi-steep roofs. The most common roof shape is [half-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_roof#Half-hip_roof)[h](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_roof#Half-hip_roof)[ipped roofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_roof#Half-hip_roof).
- Slovene is a **Slavic language** featuring the special letters Č, Š, and Ž. It is closely related to Croatian, Serbian and Montenegrin. NOTE: On top of having the same letters as Slovene, Croatian also has the letters Ć and Đ.
- Slovenian bollards are white with a black top. The front reflector will typically be **bright red**, while the back reflector will be **white**. NOTE: [Austrian](https://www.google.com/maps/@47.935857,15.6009415,3a,20.6y,357.69h,65.73t/data=!3m6!1e1!3m4!1sak-sxymIs2V63Wt8uwtlKA!2e0!7i16384!8i8192?entry=ttu) bollards have the same design, but with a dark red front reflector and a black or grey back reflector. [Montenegrin](https://maps.app.goo.gl/VKcVBeDadPfdZEwQ9) bollards also have a very similar design, also with a red and a white reflector.
- Slovenian directional signs are **yellow** with a **black border**. NOTE: Croatia, North Macedonia, Serbia and Montenegro all have similar directional signs. [Austrian](https://www.google.com/maps/@46.5568458,13.9585166,3a,17.9y,222.77h,88.58t/data=!3m6!1e1!3m4!1sgiePX811VzIqDTkzzrcHsw!2e0!7i13312!8i6656?entry=ttu) directional signs are typically white with a blue outline.
- Slovenian kilometre markers have a very distinct design. At the top left the road number is shown in a **yellow rectangle**, and a second number is to the right of it. A horizontal black line divides the top and bottom, and the distance is shown below. NOTE: [Austrian](https://goo.gl/maps/jtRvnb9JCZBuW8iLA) kilometre markers are blue with a white number.
- Slovenian chevrons usually have **red arrows** on a **white background**. You can also find a less common [version](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3rjSbRq9SMVina6a7) with a **black arrow** on a **white background**. NOTE: [Austrian](https://goo.gl/maps/BM8aPY3HC9ox4UNHA) chevrons are either white on red or red on yellow.
- Slovenian pedestrian signs have **five stripes** and a simply drawn person without a belt. NOTE: [Austrian](https://goo.gl/maps/S3nXofrbfKot9eLD6) pedestrian signs have two dotted lines, while the person on [Croatian](https://maps.app.goo.gl/yy9Gf53S8AH7Areg6) signs has a belt.
- Slovenian roads commonly feature **long outer dashes**. NOTE: Neighbouring countries do not frequently use dashed outside lines.
- Slovenian street signs are most commonly white with a **metal border** attached to signposts, or **red** and attached to house walls. NOTE: [Czech](https://goo.gl/maps/gi5f8C9LNAUumAQFA) street signs are also typically red and attached to walls. However, Czech street names will not contain the word “ulica”, which most Slovenian street names have.
- Slovenian house numbers are typically **red** with white text. NOTE: [Czech](https://www.google.com/maps/@50.7257121,15.1788819,3a,15y,1.15h,98.69t/data=!3m6!1e1!3m4!1s4rpA0yUvp4TJif4QGtcHvQ!2e0!7i16384!8i8192?entry=ttu) house numbers are also commonly red.
- Slovenia uses a regular A-type guardrail, like most European countries. This guardrail type has **rounded edges**. This is mostly useful for 50-50s with Croatia, which uses B-type guardrails. This type has square edges. NOTE: For a good overview of European guardrails, see [this infographic](/images/slovenia/europeguardrail.png).
- This type of shed, called a [kozolec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayrack), made of thin **wooden beams** with noticeable gaps in between, is very typical for Slovenia.
- A black generation 4 car can be found in Slovenia. NOTE: A black car can be found in many European countries.
- A white generation 4 Google car can also rarely be found in Slovenia. NOTE: It can also be found in other European countries.

## Regional clues

- The tallest mountains in the country can be found in the northwestern **alpine** region.
- **Flat**, or rolling, agricultural fields with **no mountains or tall hills** in view can be seen in the east of the country.
- Near the coast you get a landscape similar to the rest of the Adriatic coast; **white cliffs** with bushy vegetation, vineyards and Mediterranean architecture.
- A **wide valley** going east to west, with mountains to the north and south can be found in the southeast.
- **Italian bilingual signs** can be found in the westernmost region.

## Spotlight

- Instead of the normal red house numbers, Ljubljana will typically feature **green house numbers**. NOTE: [Street signs](https://goo.gl/maps/h5GSwk121qRJantj8) in Ljubljana are also occasionally green.
- Street signs in Celje feature the emblem of the city, a blue shield with **three yellow stars**.
- Similar to Celje, Trbovlje also features the city’s coat of arms, which is a **blue and green** shield with a black crest.
